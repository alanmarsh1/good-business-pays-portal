### Companies That Are Late Payers

Late Payers are defined here as companies that, in their own statutory disclosures, report taking more than 50 days on average to pay suppliers and paying more than 50% of invoices late, based on data published under the UK government’s Payment Practices and Performance reporting regime.

The number of companies falling into this category has fallen by around 16% year-on-year, from 263 companies in February 2025 to 223 in February 2026. This is the lowest number of companies meeting this definition on our Late & Slow Watchlist since we began publishing this analysis in 2023.

For the first time, changes to the reporting regime mean companies must disclose not only the proportion of invoices paid late, but also the monetary value of those late payments.

Analysis of companies’ own publicly reported data for the six-month  
period June–December 2025 shows that more than £8.75 billion of invoices were paid late by companies on this list. Of that total, over £5 billion related to invoices where companies reported no dispute.

Despite the overall reduction in numbers, 122 companies still report paying 70% or more of their invoices late, compared with 150 companies at the same point last year.

Alongside each company, we also indicate how many times it has appeared on our Late & Slow Watchlist since reporting began, to provide context on whether these results reflect a persistent pattern or a single reporting period.

Methodology note:  
This analysis is based solely on information published by companies under the UK government’s Payment Practices and Performance reporting requirements. The rankings reflect companies’ own reported averages and percentages for the relevant reporting periods. Inclusion in this list does not imply illegality or breach of contract but indicates the payment outcomes companies themselves have disclosed.

### **Companies That Are Slow Payers**

Slow Payers are defined here as companies that, in their own statutory disclosures, report an average time to pay suppliers of more than 80 days, based on data published under the UK government’s Payment Practices and Performance reporting regime.

As of February 2026, 83 companies report average payment times of more than 80 days, below the peak of 127 companies recorded in August 2025, but remaining the roughly the same as this time last year.

At the extreme end of the scale, 29 companies now report taking 100 days or more on average to pay suppliers, up from 27 companies in February 2025. While the increase is modest, it suggests that a core group of companies continues to operate with exceptionally long payment times.
