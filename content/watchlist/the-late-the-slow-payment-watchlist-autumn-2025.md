Good Business Pays have now been publishing our Late & Slow Watchlist since the beginning of 2023, to provide a list of companies who are reporting the worst payment performance figures as part of the Government’s Payment Practices & Performance Regulations. 

This list continues to provide useful insights for anyone hoping to work with these companies. In a year where the UK is preparing to toughen up regulations around late payment, it will be interesting to see if the regular names on these lists change payment behaviour.

We update our Late & Slow Watchlist every six months. Why six months? Because the regulations state that large companies must report their payment times every six months. In this way we can track payment culture over time and see who the serial late payers are.

We feel it is important to make this information available for anyone to see and have been delighted that a growing number of large companies are responding to calls for improvement and making big and positive changes to their payment performance. Please check our [ _Achievements_](<https://goodbusinesspays.com/our-achievements/>) page to find out more.

Analysing data from every company that reported payment performance between 1st January 2025 and 15th September 2025, Good Business Pays identified:

  * **The Late Payers:** Who report paying more than 50% of their invoices later than agreed terms ** _as well as_****** report taking more than 50 days (average) to pay their invoices.
  * **The Slow Payers:** Who report an average time to pay of more than 80 days.
  * **Serial Late Payers:** Who have reported paying more than 50% of invoices late **every year** since reporting began in 2018, demonstrating the culture of late and slow payment is engrained. 

**Key Insights: September 2025**

**Late Payers**** _(Companies that report taking more than 50 days on average to pay AND pay more than 50% of their invoices late)_**

The number of companies falling into this group remains roughly the same as a year ago with 297 companies this year versus 285 a year ago.

There has been a big increase in the number of companies reporting paying 70% or more of their invoices late, from 122 a year ago to 150 now.
