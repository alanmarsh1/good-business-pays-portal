**UK companies cut average payment times from 44.4 days to 20.9 and late invoices from 58% to 12% — years ahead of new government legislation — as Good Business Pays publishes its Top 100 Most Improved Payers list**

Good Business Pays, the independent organisation behind the annual Fast Payer Awards, today publishes its Top 100 Most Improved Payers list, marking six years since it began independently measuring and publishing how promptly the UK's largest companies pay their suppliers.

The list, drawn from companies' own government-reported payment practice data, ranks the 100 UK businesses that have made the greatest improvement in payment speed and reliability since Good Business Pays began tracking payment behaviour in 2019. Across the companies it monitors, the average time taken to pay an invoice has more than halved, from 44.4 days to 20.9, while the proportion of invoices paid late has fallen from 58% to 12% — a 46-percentage-point improvement.

Individual transformations within the list are striking. FCB Europe Limited, ranked 4th, has cut its average payment time from 135 days to 19. Crown Chicken Limited, which tops the ranking, reduced its late-payment rate from 98% of invoices to 18% while cutting payment time nearly fourfold. Man Fund Management UK Limited now pays suppliers, on average, on the day it receives an invoice. The list spans construction, food production, financial services, retail and the charity sector, including five separate McLaren Construction operating entities, Starling Bank, Cranswick Country Foods, and charities including Leonard Cheshire Disability and the Stroke Association.

Crucially, every company on the list made these improvements voluntarily, ahead of any legal requirement to do so. The Commercial Payments Bill currently making its way through Parliament, is described by ministers as the largest crackdown on late payment in over 25 years. Once in force, it will introduce a statutory 60-day cap on payment terms for large firms paying smaller suppliers, mandatory interest on late payments at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, and new powers for the Small Business Commissioner to investigate and fine persistent late payers. The companies recognised in this list achieved their improvements before that legislation existed.

**Terry Corby, Founder / CEO, Good Business Pays, said:**

"_We started this six years ago with an idea, that if you measure how companies actually treat their suppliers, and publish it honestly, most of them will choose to do better rather than be named as one of the worst. This list is the proof. These hundred companies didn 't wait for a law to tell them to pay on time, they did it because it was visible, measured, and it mattered to the suppliers deciding who to work with. Regulation is coming, and it's needed. But recognition got there first._"

The full list, including every company's before-and-after payment figures and methodology, is available [here](<https://goodbusinesspays.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Top_100_Most_Improved_Payers_2.pdf>).

**Notes on the data**

  * Figures are drawn from companies' own government-reported payment practice data (Reporting on Payment Practices and Performance Regulations 2017). Non-trading holding companies, project SPVs and intra-group entities are excluded.
  * Companies are ranked by a combined score reflecting the reduction in both average payment time and share of invoices paid late, comparing each company's first reported year against its most recent.
  * Source for legislative detail: Department for Business and Trade, "Largest crackdown on late payments in over 25 years as landmark Bill enters Parliament", published 19 May 2026, gov.uk.
